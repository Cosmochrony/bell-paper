This repository contains the source of the [paper on non-injective projection and Bell correlations](pdf/Bell.pdf).

This work studies a class of **effective models** in which the mapping between underlying configurations and observable
outcomes is **non-injective**. We show that, in such settings, **Bell-type correlations** can arise as a structural
property of effective descriptions: **probabilistic factorization fails at the level of observables** even when the
underlying description is local and causally well-defined.

The paper does not introduce new dynamical laws. Its goal is to isolate a **minimal descriptive condition** under which
Bell inequalities do not apply, thereby clarifying what precisely is constrained by Bell-type no-go results.

## Core Claims

The paper is organized around the following statements:

1. **Bell inequalities are constraints on factorizable effective descriptions**  
   Bell-type derivations require a factorization assumption on observable joint probabilities (conditional on an
   underlying parameterization).
2. **Non-injective projection generically obstructs factorization**  
   When multiple underlying configurations map to the same observable description, conditioning on any effective
   parameter typically does not isolate independent local contributions, and the factorizable form fails.
3. **Violations do not imply dynamical superluminality**  
   The failure of factorization is a structural feature of the descriptive mapping and does not require superluminal
   influences, retrocausality, or ad hoc nonlocal dynamics.
4. **Classical behavior is recovered in approximately injective regimes**  
   In regimes where environmental interactions and coarse-graining render the effective mapping approximately injective,
   factorization becomes a good approximation and Bell violations are suppressed in practice.

## What This Paper Does Not Assume

To avoid conflating structural and ontological claims, the paper does not assume:

- a specific underlying ontology
- a modification of quantum mechanics
- retrocausal mechanisms
- superdeterminism
- nonlocal dynamical influences

The analysis is phrased in terms of **effective descriptions**, probability assignments, and the structure of mappings
from underlying configurations to observables.
