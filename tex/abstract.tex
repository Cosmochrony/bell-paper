\abstract{Violations of Bell inequalities are commonly interpreted as evidence for
nonlocal influences or as a constraint on realist descriptions. We show that
Bell-type violations arise generically when the mapping from underlying
configurations to observable outcomes is non-injective. In this setting, the
effective description fails to support probabilistic factorization, not because
of superluminal dynamics, but due to structural information loss under
projection. The framework preserves locality at the underlying level, requires
no hidden-variable dynamics, and does not modify quantum mechanics. We identify
a minimal structural condition for Bell violations and clarify the regime in
which classical factorizability is recovered when the effective map becomes
approximately injective.}
