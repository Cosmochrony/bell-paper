\abstract{
  Violations of Bell inequalities are commonly interpreted as evidence
  for nonlocal influences or as constraints on realist descriptions.
  We show that the failure of Bell-type factorizability arises generically
  whenever observable outcomes are obtained through a non-injective mapping
  from an underlying configuration space.
  In this framework, probabilistic factorization fails not because of
  superluminal dynamics or hidden causal influences, but as a structural
  consequence of information loss under projection.
  Observable outcomes correspond to equivalence classes of underlying
  configurations, preventing the assignment of independent local variables.
  Such non-injective mappings arise naturally when effective observables are
  defined as projections of higher-dimensional relational configuration spaces
  with non-trivial fiber structure.
  We demonstrate explicitly, via a toy model, that this mechanism reproduces
  Bell--CHSH violations while preserving operational no-signalling and
  statistical independence of measurement settings.
  The framework preserves locality at the underlying level, introduces no
  additional hidden-variable dynamics, and does not modify quantum mechanics.
  It identifies a minimal structural condition for Bell violations and clarifies
  how classical factorizability is recovered in regimes where the effective
  mapping becomes approximately injective.
}
