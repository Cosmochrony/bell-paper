\abstract{
  Violations of Bell inequalities are commonly interpreted as evidence
  for nonlocal influences or as constraints on realist descriptions.
  We show that the failure of Bell-type factorizability arises generically
  whenever observable outcomes are obtained through a non-injective mapping
  from an underlying configuration space.
  In this setting, probabilistic factorization fails not because of
  superluminal dynamics but due to structural information loss under
  projection.
  Such non-injective mappings may naturally arise when observable
  descriptions correspond to projections of higher-dimensional relational
  configuration spaces with non-trivial fiber structure.
  The framework preserves locality at the underlying level, requires no
  hidden-variable dynamics, and does not modify quantum mechanics.
  We identify a minimal structural condition for Bell violations and
  clarify the regime in which classical factorizability is recovered when
  the effective mapping becomes approximately injective.
}
