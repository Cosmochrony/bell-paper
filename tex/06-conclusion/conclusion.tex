\section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  We have shown that violations of Bell inequalities can arise as a purely structural
  feature of effective physical descriptions when the mapping from underlying
  configurations to observable outcomes is non-injective.
  In such cases, the probabilistic factorization assumptions required for Bell-type
  derivations fail at the level of observables, even though locality and causal
  independence are preserved at the underlying level.

  This analysis clarifies that Bell inequalities constrain a class of descriptive
  models rather than fundamental dynamics.
  They apply only when observable outcomes admit an injective or factorizable
  representation in terms of underlying variables.
  When this condition is not satisfied, Bell-type correlations may emerge without
  invoking nonlocal interactions, retrocausality, or hidden variables.

  We have further shown how classical behavior naturally arises in regimes where
  environmental interactions and coarse-graining render the effective projection
  approximately injective.
  In this limit, joint probabilities recover classical factorization and Bell
  inequalities are no longer violated in practice.

  The present work does not propose a modification of quantum mechanics or a new
  ontological framework.
  Its contribution is to isolate a minimal and sufficient structural condition for
  the failure of Bell inequalities, thereby offering a clarified conceptual
  perspective on the origin of quantum correlations.

  We conclude that Bell inequality violations need not be interpreted as evidence for
  fundamental nonlocality, but may instead reflect intrinsic limitations of effective
  descriptions in which observable outcomes are treated as independently specifiable
  local properties.
